Research

I am currently wrapping up my PhD in Physics with Dr. Damon Clark at Yale University, where I use 2-photon microscopy and computational modelling to investigate the fly's sense of direction, which it uses as a compass to navigate the world. I am interested in how the brain forms internal representations of the world to guide navigation and decision making. I am excited to dive deeper into higher cognitive behavior tasks and learn new statistical modelling techniques.

Previously, I worked on membrane biophysics and super-resolution microscopy with Dr. Helge Ewers at Freie Universität Berlin and Dr. Pina Colarusso at the University of Calgary, and computational research on critical spreading processes on neural networks and bipartite stock-trader networks with Dr. Jörn Davidsen at the University of Calgary and Dr. Siew Ann Cheong at NTU Singapore.

Current work · Yale

The fly's internal compass

Flies keep track of which way they're facing with a compass-like population of neurons that holds a persistent estimate of heading. That estimate gets updated by the fly's own movements and anchored by visual landmarks. To investigate this circuit, I performed my lab's first simultaneous recordings of behavior and neural population activity by constructing a treadmill for flies and 3D 2p imaging tools. Together with computational models, I hope to better understand how visual input shapes this internal representation of direction. Paper coming soon!
